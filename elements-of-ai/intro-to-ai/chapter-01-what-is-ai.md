# Elements of AI — Chapter 1: What Is AI?

## 1. Key Concepts & Takeaways

### Why There Is No Single Fixed Definition ("The AI Effect")
- As soon as an algorithm or technique becomes routine and well understood (for example, route optimization, spam filtering, or GPS navigation), people tend to stop calling it "AI" and treat it as ordinary software.
- **Moravec's Paradox:** Tasks that feel difficult for humans (playing chess, arithmetic, checking millions of route permutations) follow explicit rules and are easy for computers, whereas tasks effortless for a toddler (grasping objects, navigating messy rooms, reading social context) are hard for machines.

### The Field Hierarchy
- **Computer Science** $\supset$ **Artificial Intelligence** $\supset$ **Machine Learning** $\supset$ **Deep Learning**
- **Data Science** overlaps heavily with Machine Learning and Statistics while extending into data collection, cleaning, analysis, and communication.
- **Machine Learning:** Systems that improve performance on a specific task through experience or data.
- **Deep Learning:** A subfield of ML using multi-layer neural networks to learn complex representations.

### Philosophy of AI
- **The Turing Test (Alan Turing):** Evaluates whether a machine's conversational behavior in a blind chat is indistinguishable from a human's. *Limitation:* fooling a human judge via deflection or humor is not equivalent to genuine understanding.
- **The Chinese Room Argument (John Searle):** Following syntactic rules flawlessly (strong behavioral output) does not prove semantic understanding or consciousness.
- **Narrow AI vs. General AI (AGI):** Real-world deployed AI is narrow—trained for bounded tasks under human supervision and programming constraints—not self-adapting general intelligence.

## 2. Critique of Common Definitions & Working Definition

### Critiquing Three Common Formulations
1. **"Cool things that computers can't do":** Too narrow and subjective. It excludes working AI systems already deployed today (chess engines, recommendation systems) while accidentally including purely human capabilities like forming genuine emotional bonds.
2. **"Machines imitating intelligent human behavior":** Misses how modern algorithms actually work. Recommendation engines (such as streaming music/video platforms) optimize thousands of statistical parameters without imitating human thought or understanding real-life emotional context.
3. **"Autonomous and adaptive systems":** Both too broad and too sci-fi. A rule-based chess engine cannot wake up tomorrow and drive a car; deployed systems operate as closed models requiring human design, programming, and supervision.

### Working Definition
> **AI is a collection of human-designed computer methods that use data, rules, or statistical patterns to solve specific complex problems with limited autonomy or learning.**

This avoids anthropomorphism, keeps focus on real computational mechanisms (rules, statistical pattern matching, optimization), and clearly distinguishes narrow engineering systems from living organisms or science fiction.
